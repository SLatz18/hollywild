# Hollywild: The City That Answers

This is the authored campaign implemented by `content/campaign.json`, rather than the earlier design proposal in `WORLD_AND_MISSIONS.md`.

The first-time completion target is approximately 120 minutes. This is an unmeasured pacing estimate, not an observed playthrough result. All 24 missions form one sequential investigation, while all five districts and the new public interiors remain open. Begin at the campaign desk in the garage. The journal names the current mission and the exact room and object for the next step. Completing a mission activates its successor where you are; there is no compulsory garage turn-in loop.

The story follows the attempt to restore Aurora as a network people can operate and repair. Slush has a credible fear of another freeze, real repair costs, and a harmful private estate proposal. He concedes after public records, a lawful access agreement, independent measurements, an actual backup path, and a staged branch fault show a better answer. His settlement preserves verified repair debt without evicting residents. The final emphasis changes what the city invests in first.

## Persistent choices and later consequences

All options are viable. There is no secret morality score or failure ending.

| Policy, mission 10 | 1 RESERVE; 2 SHARED | Mission 12 explains reserve replacement versus pooled allocation. Mission 20 uses the matching ledger response for the staged seal replacement. |
| Trust, mission 14 | 1 REVIEW; 2 PUBLIC | Mission 16 changes why Slush accepts the drawing. Mission 21 preserves joint review privacy or witnessed published totals. Tenant names stay private under both choices. |
| Ending, mission 23 | 1 WARMTH; 2 WORK; 3 COMMONS | Slush offers roofs, training space, or hall access in mission 23. Mission 24 reacts before the corresponding warmth, industry, or commons epilogue. |

The ending selection occurs before the final mission, allowing later spoken consequences. The actual epilogue is awarded only after mission 24 completion. All endings restore basic service, preserve access, honor the measured service limit, and return to free play. The three differences are first investment priorities: homes/clinic, repair work/training, or shared halls/kitchens.

The Slush reconciliation is earned through mandatory evidence in all routes: public ownership, valid easement, independent regulator tests, a fault-isolating bypass, a second message path, and a transparent debt settlement. Optional lore adds his earlier greenhouse payment without becoming necessary to understand or finish the arc.

## Content and pacing budget

Authored dialogue, wrong-answer explanations, alternate outcomes, conditional reactions, lore, and endings contain approximately 12,189 words, excluding objective hints, labels, and metadata. The invariant main path plus the first option of each choice contains 8,464 words before selected later reactions and its ending. A route selecting HIGH, RESERVE, REVIEW, and WARMTH reads approximately 8,822 main-story words including its reactions and ending, without optional lore or wrong-answer retries. The 40 optional lore entries add 1,712 words. All three epilogues total 462 words. Aggregate counts across mutually exclusive paths must not be presented as required reading.

There are 24 missions with six meaningful steps each: 144 required interactions, including 45 clue puzzles and four persistent choices. There are no consecutive same-object interactions within a mission. The authored data contains 1,215 short pages across all paths, wrong answers, lore, and endings. That is not the required page count for one playthrough. Automatic handheld wrapping may add display pages. Text requires active page advances; no enforced waiting was authored to lengthen play.

The expanded puzzles include two-constraint parcel repacking, whole-batch output planning with an exact demand and reserved stock, load versus route capacity, remaining carrying margin, independent sensor comparison, maintenance prerequisites, easement geometry, privacy-preserving financial review, signal chronology, two-fault path topology, branch-load headroom, and exception handling in a launch packet. The new steps use existing room objects and authored menu logic. They do not imply a new simulated factory, inventory, vehicle, or timing system.

## Economy and persistence boundaries

The story grants 1,008 Coins and 48 Salvage across 24 guarded one-time rewards. Individual rewards are 36, 40, 44, or 48 Coins, plus 2 Salvage. No main-story objective spends either resource. Purchases and repeatable jobs remain optional. Existing wallet and storage limits still apply; a pending completion must remain pending if its payout cannot fit, or if a repeatable job is active. These are engine transaction rules, not a second story economy.

Quest badges, slips, maps, parts, permissions, cards, and launch packets are flags. They cannot be sold or consumed. Ordinary property restoration remains a separate progression goal; the final campaign does not depend on a 1,000-Coin property threshold. Optional node lore is remembered without a payout or productive tick. A mission interaction is not a repeatable contract completion and must not run ordinary production.

Each mission advances one step per interaction even if the next objective shares its site. The story journal and persistent mission/step fields determine the current destination after a checkpoint. Saved route, policy, trust, ending, and lore flags preserve reactions. The engine validation and compiled native build, rather than this narrative document, establish runtime behavior and safety.
